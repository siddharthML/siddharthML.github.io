01Why Online Evals Are the Enterprise Default

Most teams reach the point where they need to evaluate an AI product without a neat golden dataset. What they do have is often more valuable: production traces.

Every user interaction leaves a record of the request, model behavior, tool calls, retrieved data, intermediate steps and final response. Offline evaluation tests a fixed, versioned dataset before release. Online evaluation observes the system with real users, real data and real ambiguity. Enterprises need both, but rarely begin with the offline kind: golden datasets are expensive to build and age quickly, while production traces accumulate every day.

Realism

Actual behavior

Traces preserve messy, multi-step and edge-case interactions that dataset designers did not anticipate.

Freshness

Current conditions

Live traffic keeps evaluation aligned with new features, user behavior and emerging failure modes.

Decision relevance

Business outcomes

Online evals answer whether the product is doing what users and the business need now.

The catch is that traces are raw material, not evaluation. Trustworthy signals require deliberate sampling, judging, human review and governance.

02Start From Traces, Not Metrics

You cannot evaluate what you cannot see, and you cannot improve what you have not understood.

A trace is the complete record of one execution—from the initial request to the final response. Capturing only the answer leaves the cause of failure unknowable: missing data, a retrieval filter, a skipped tool, a routing choice and an upstream timeout can all produce the same bad output.

Complete, structured instrumentation should span routing, agents, tools, retrieval and the model. Every trace needs stable identifiers for environment, session, user, tenant, model, prompt version, agent and tool. Latency, token counts, tool arguments, retrieved document IDs and filters are nearly free to log at execution time and expensive or impossible to reconstruct later.

03The Core Discipline: Error Analysis

The most important evaluation activity is not writing evaluators. It is the human-led work of reading real traces, writing open-ended notes, clustering those notes into failure categories and refining the categories until new traces stop revealing new modes of failure.

  1. Gather a diverse pool. Start with production data and supplement it with synthetic examples where traffic is thin or edge cases are rare.
  2. Read traces yourself first. Annotate at least 30 before asking an LLM to help; early suggestions bias what you notice.
  3. Record the first failure. Upstream errors cascade, so the first failure is usually the one that matters.
  4. Cluster the notes. Let an LLM propose groupings only after the manual pass, then review every cluster.
  5. Relabel and repeat. Continue until the taxonomy reaches saturation and new traces stop changing it.

Generic helpfulness, coherence and hallucination scores may measure qualities unrelated to the product’s actual failures. Error analysis produces a taxonomy in the language of the product, where every metric maps to an owner who can fix the underlying problem.

Worked exampleWhat a Production-Grade Taxonomy Looks Like

A practical taxonomy uses a small set of memorable level-one families and precise, actionable level-two codes. It is diagnostic rather than merely evaluative: a code should explain why the system failed and route the case to the right owner.

Principle 01

Diagnostic, not decorative

A label must explain why the response failed and direct the case to someone who can act.

Principle 02

Domain semantic

Granularity, scenarios, versions and publication cycles matter more than generic AI-quality language.

Principle 03

Versioned like code

Record the taxonomy version on every score so historical comparisons remain meaningful.

How to derive your own taxonomy

  • Open-code at least 30 traces before involving an LLM and describe the first failure from the user’s perspective.
  • Review every proposed cluster; superficially similar failures may belong to entirely different owners.
  • Maintain roughly 100 diverse traces for the human-agent loop and revisit the taxonomy after material product, prompt, model or tool changes.
  • Review small outlier-biased batches between major changes and name an owner for every failure family.

04Sampling: The Art of Choosing What to Evaluate

You cannot evaluate every trace, and a single sampling strategy will blind you. Run several samples in parallel and record the selection reason on every evaluated trace so unlike pass rates are never silently conflated.

Interactive sampling portfolio

Five samples, five different claims

Select a sample to see what it reveals and what can go wrong when its result is interpreted without context.

Selection is metadata

Automation-assisted sampling

Semantic stratification groups traces by similarity and samples across clusters, protecting distinct usage patterns from being drowned out by the most common query. Active learning uses new human labels to select the next most informative traces: likely failures, uncertain judge decisions, cases similar to a new failure and cases unlike anything already labeled.

Practical sizing: use roughly 100 diverse traces for the initial human-agent loop; expect about 100–200 labeled examples per failure mode to calibrate an LLM judge across training, development and held-out test sets; keep a recurring regression suite of 100+ high-value examples small enough to run frequently.

Whenever a judge is used, return a structured sub-code, evidence quoted from the trace, the responsible component and whether a required invocation was missing. Evidence makes a verdict auditable and a human override efficient.

Judge disciplineCalibrating LLM Judges Against Humans

An LLM judge is only as useful as its measured agreement with the people whose judgment it automates. Scope rubrics narrowly and prefer binary-leaning decisions to subjective 1–5 scales.

Define narrow rubric
Label 100–200 examples
Split train, dev and test
Measure TPR, TNR and confusion
Recalibrate after change

The confusion matrix is the most useful artifact because it reveals which categories the judge systematically conflates—usually a sign of rubric ambiguity or missing trace evidence. Watch conceptually close pairs such as data never loaded versus a current cycle not yet published, or granularity not modelled versus the wrong granularity returned.

The production model can usually judge scoped binary classifications if it agrees with expert labels. Establish agreement first with the most capable judge available; for high-stakes decisions, consider a different provider because related models can share blind spots.

Do not write evaluator crashes or parse failures back as confident diagnoses. Exclude execution failures from quality metrics and make score identifiers deterministic—such as trace ID plus evaluator name—so reruns are idempotent and never create conflicting duplicate scores.

06The Human Layer: Small, Expert and Empowered

LLM judges scale judgment; they do not replace it. Human experts define the taxonomy, label calibration and regression data, resolve ambiguity, audit judge drift and discover new failure modes. Evaluation is a sensemaking process, not a static target that can be fully delegated.

For a focused product, a single domain expert can serve as the quality decision-maker and eliminate annotation conflicts. Multi-domain programs may need several annotators: label independently, measure agreement with a chance-corrected metric such as Cohen’s Kappa, then adjudicate disagreements and turn each one into a rubric refinement.

What the expert sees

A domain-shaped review surface

  • Render outputs as users experience them
  • Collapse raw technical detail behind disclosure controls
  • Support keyboard-driven pass/fail labeling
  • Show progress and evaluator verdicts alongside the trace

What the expert can do

One-click quality actions

  • Add a case to the regression set
  • File a bug with trace evidence
  • Re-run an evaluator
  • Correct the label and improve the rubric

Engineers identify retrieval, tool and integration failures; product managers and domain experts identify unmet expectations and missing capabilities. Over time, consolidate error analysis under the domain expert and show real outcomes—not merely whether a tool returned a success status. Avoid outsourcing core annotation to reviewers without product context. Automate the loop around the expert, not the expert’s judgment.

07Evaluators Are Not Guardrails—and Vice Versa

These systems have opposite operational requirements. Guardrails run synchronously in the request path and must be fast, deterministic and simple. Evaluators run after the fact and can afford heavier computation because they feed dashboards, regression suites and improvement loops rather than blocking the immediate response.

Interactive responsibility split

Same evidence, different job

Switch between the two systems to compare their timing, constraints and operational result.

Do not conflate them

A deterministic check may serve both roles only when it meets the latency and false-positive budget of the live path. A slow or non-deterministic LLM judge almost never belongs there.

Do not let evaluators silently correct production responses. Classification confidence is not safe remediation. Limit automatic corrections to narrow, reversible cases with deterministic validation. Set the false-positive versus false-negative trade-off explicitly for every use case before automating.

08Closing the Loop: From Production Insight to Release Confidence

Online evaluation discovers failure modes. Confirmed failures become labeled cases. Labeled cases become regression tests. Regression tests become release gates.

Interactive continuous quality loop

Every failure should improve the release system

Select a stage to follow evidence from production observation to verified improvement.

Evidence compounds
  • Reproduce before generalizing. Reduce a failure to the simplest case that still breaks; use multi-turn tests only when context is causal.
  • Add confirmed failures to CI. Production monitoring exposes what the offline suite missed; make every confirmed mode durable.
  • Prefer deterministic CI evals. Curated pre-release suites run often; reserve costly judges for production samples without references.
  • Record lineage everywhere. Tie every score to its trace, judge, taxonomy, prompt, model and run versions.

Release gates must be risk-based rather than global: no regression on authorization or data integrity, minimum success per critical workflow, no material degradation for protected segments, and latency and cost within budget. Every gate needs a dataset, metric, threshold, owner and expiring exception process.

09Metrics That Roll Up Without Washing Out

Executives want one number; engineers need a hundred. Serve both with a metric hierarchy, not a composite score.

Executive

Business confidence

Critical workflow success, severe incident rate, adoption and cost per successful task.

Product

Experience quality

Success and failure rates by use case, segment, workflow and tenant.

Engineering

Root cause

Failure distributions, tool transitions, latency, tokens, cost and regressions.

Evaluation health

Signal quality

Judge agreement, taxonomy coverage, fallback and ambiguity rates, backlog and drift.

Every rollup must drill down to traces and the sample definition behind the number. Guard relentlessly against selection bias, where only pre-flagged failures are evaluated, and aggregate masking, where a healthy average hides a broken tenant or critical low-volume workflow. Multi-tenant platforms need segmented datasets, dashboards, thresholds and access controls.

10Governance: The Unglamorous Prerequisite

At enterprise scale, the evaluation system is production infrastructure and must be governed like it.

01

Data protection

Apply redaction, retention, role-based access and tenant isolation to traces, reports and notifications.

02

Evaluator governance

Version every rubric, calibration record and approved-use statement; define explicit prohibitions.

03

Auditability

Attribute label overrides, taxonomy revisions, release exceptions and threshold changes.

04

Evaluator health

Monitor agreement, fallbacks, parse failures and evidence completeness separately from product quality.

A judge model update, prompt regression or instrumentation gap can silently corrupt every score. The evaluation layer needs its own operational monitoring.

11Failure Modes to Expect—and Preempt

Automation bias

Treating judge output as ground truth without measured expert agreement.

Vanity pass rates

Optimizing for green dashboards instead of evaluators that expose real issues.

Metric sprawl

Building evaluators for imagined failures before doing error analysis on observed ones.

Synthetic dependence

Using synthetic data to infer production prevalence or represent complex real-world edges.

Trace blindness

Judging final answers without retrieval, tool-call and intermediate execution evidence.

Frozen taxonomies

Allowing product evolution to outrun the categories, owners and rubrics used to measure it.

ConclusionEvaluation as the Operating System of Product Quality

Online evaluation without golden references is not a degraded benchmark. It is how mature enterprises understand AI systems in real conditions: capture complete traces; let human error analysis define a precise, owned and versioned taxonomy; sample deliberately; use the cheapest evaluator that works; calibrate every judge against experts; and turn every confirmed failure into a test whose fix is verified in production.

The traces you already collect are the dataset—provided you treat them with discipline.

Done well, evaluation stops being a reporting exercise after incidents. It becomes a continuous quality system that shows how the product behaves, whether it is safe to ship and whether it is actually improving. Quality compounds with every release because the evidence loop remembers what the organization has learned.
